Pac-Man in pure hardware · no CPU · no frame buffer

Maze, four ghosts with their own chase rules, power pellets, fruit, breakable walls and five levels, all computed in SystemVerilog as the screen is scanned. What you play here is not a re-implementation: it is the actual RTL, simulated with Verilator and compiled to WebAssembly, with its display signals sampled into the canvas the way a monitor would.

On the real hardware

How this works

  1. The game core is the same RTL that is synthesized for the Tang Nano boards. It is checked frame by frame against a golden run recorded from the original DE10-Standard design.
  2. Verilator compiles it to C++, and Emscripten compiles that to WebAssembly. A Web Worker runs the design and samples its display signals (RGB, data enable, VSYNC) into the canvas.
  3. A frame is about 320,000 clock cycles. Rows that would play out exactly as in the previous frame are replayed from a saved snapshot of every register; the rest (around Pac-Man and the ghosts, changed tiles, the one-second tick) are clocked cycle by cycle. A test runs this beside the fully clocked model and compares every register and every pixel of every frame. Clock every row to see the difference.
  4. Your keys become the same 8-button mask the board receives over USB-serial.
